ATS-friendly resume template you can copy

The most ATS-friendly resume is the plainest one: one column, standard headings, real text, consistent dates. Copy the skeleton below into any editor, then follow the notes for each section. Design can come later; structure comes first.

Keep it in this order for most roles. A new graduate can move Education above Experience; everything else stays.

Contact line and summary

Contact. Put your name on its own line in the body of the page — not in a header. City and state are enough; a full street address adds nothing. Use a plain email address and a LinkedIn URL you have customised.

Target title. One line, written the way the posting writes it: “Senior Data Analyst”, not “Data Wizard”. This gives the parser and the recruiter the same signal.

Summary. Two or three lines, no first-person pronouns. A pattern that works:

“Data analyst with 5 years in e-commerce reporting. Builds SQL pipelines and Tableau dashboards used by marketing and finance. Cut weekly reporting time by 60% by automating a manual Excel process.”

Experience bullets that work for people and parsers

Each role starts with a line the parser can split: Title — Employer, Location, Dates. Then three to six bullets. Write each bullet as:

PartExample
Action verbLaunched, Reduced, Negotiated, Built, Trained
Whata self-serve onboarding flow
How / tool (keyword)using Figma and Amplitude
Resultraising week-one activation from 31% to 44%

If you do not have numbers, use scope instead: team size, number of clients, frequency (“weekly”), or who used the output (“adopted by 3 regional teams”). Avoid bullets that only list duties (“Responsible for…”).

Keep older roles short. Anything more than ten to fifteen years back can be one line each, or grouped as “Earlier experience”.

Skills, education and certifications

Skills. Group skills by category on plain lines: “Languages: Python, SQL, R” / “Tools: Tableau, dbt, Airflow”. No skill bars, stars or percentage meters — they are images or shapes, and a parser cannot read a level from a picture.

Education. Degree, major, school, and year. Add GPA only if it is strong and you are early in your career. Relevant coursework is useful for new graduates.

Certifications. Write the full name and the acronym once (“Project Management Professional (PMP)”). Many recruiters search for the acronym; some systems search for the full name.

Tailor one copy per job

  1. Save the template as your master resume with every role and every bullet you could use.
  2. For each application, duplicate it and name the copy after the job.
  3. Highlight the required skills in the job post. Make sure each appears at least once, in context, where true.
  4. Reorder bullets so the most relevant ones come first under each role.
  5. Delete what does not help this application, so the copy stays at one or two pages.
  6. Export, then run the paste test: copy all text from the PDF into a plain editor and check the order.

Frequently asked questions

Are Word or Google Docs templates ATS-friendly?

Many are not, because they use tables or two columns for layout. Check by pasting the exported text into a plain editor: if the order is wrong, the ATS will likely see it wrong too.

Can I use colour on an ATS-friendly resume?

Yes. Colour on headings or a thin accent line does not affect text extraction. What matters is that the words are real text in a single column.

Should I put a photo on my resume?

In the US and UK, generally no. Some countries expect one; follow local norms. In CV Desk the header photo is optional and off by default.

Does this template guarantee an interview?

No template can. It removes common parsing problems so your experience is read as written; the content and fit with the role still decide the rest.

No checklist, template or score can promise that a resume passes a particular employer’s applicant tracking system. Every employer configures its own system and its own screening questions.

Resume vs CV: what is the difference, and which one do you need?

In the US and Canada, a resume is a one-to-two-page summary tailored to a job, and a CV is a long, complete record used mainly for academic, research and medical roles. In the UK, Ireland, most of Europe and much of Asia, “CV” is simply the everyday word for what Americans call a resume. The right choice depends on the country and the type of job — not on which word sounds more professional.

Norms vary by employer and sector, so when a posting gives instructions, those instructions win.

When to use a resume

  • Applying to most private-sector jobs in the United States or Canada.
  • Any posting that says “resume” and gives a page limit.
  • Industry roles for PhDs leaving academia — convert the CV to a two-page resume focused on skills and results.
  • Online applications that run through an applicant tracking system, where a concise, single-column document with standard headings is read most reliably.

When to use a CV

  • Any job application in the UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, most of Europe, the Middle East and much of Asia — here “CV” means a two-page job document, written like a US resume.
  • Academic positions anywhere: faculty, postdoc, research fellow, graduate school, grants.
  • Medical and scientific roles that ask for a full record of training, publications and presentations.
  • Some international organisations and public bodies that specify a CV or a standard form.

An academic CV usually adds: Research interests, Publications, Presentations, Grants and funding, Teaching, Awards, Professional service, References.

How to convert a CV into a resume (and back)

CV → resume (for industry or US roles)

  1. Write a three-line summary aimed at the target job title.
  2. Turn research projects into Experience entries with results: methods used, tools, what changed.
  3. Replace the full publication list with one line (“12 peer-reviewed papers; 2 as first author”) or a short “Selected publications” section.
  4. Add a Skills section using the job description’s wording.
  5. Cut to two pages, single column.

Resume → UK/EU CV

  1. Rename the document “CV” and use British spelling for UK roles.
  2. Two pages is normal; you can keep a little more detail on older roles.
  3. Check local norms on photos and personal details — and leave them out unless the employer expects them.

Format rules that apply to both

  • Single column, standard headings, consistent dates.
  • Real text, not an image or a scanned page.
  • Contact details in the body of the page, not in the document header.
  • Name the file clearly: Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf or Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CV longer than a resume?

An academic CV is, because it lists every publication and talk. A UK or European job CV is usually two pages — about the same as an experienced US resume.

Can I send a CV when a US job asks for a resume?

Send what they ask for. A long academic CV in a US industry application often gets skimmed or misparsed; a two-page resume focused on the role reads better.

What is a Europass CV?

A standard CV format published by the European Union. Some public bodies and programmes request it; many private employers accept a shorter, custom CV instead.

Should a UK CV include a photo?

Generally no. Leave it out unless the employer specifically asks for one.
